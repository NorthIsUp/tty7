---
title: "The hotkey window"
description: "A system-wide hotkey shows and hides a dedicated tty7 window from any app."
---

<kbd>⌥ Space</kbd> shows the hotkey window, a tty7 window of its own, from any
app. Press it again while that window has focus and it hides, handing focus
back to the app you were in. If it is on screen but something else has focus,
the hotkey raises it.

Only the hotkey shows it. <kbd>⌘ Tab</kbd>, a Dock click and a relaunch bring
back your other tty7 windows and leave the hotkey window hidden, and the
hotkey leaves those other windows where they are. The first press opens it
with a fresh workspace; its tabs are kept like any other window's and come
back on the next press after a restart. When its workspace is the only one,
launching tty7 or clicking its Dock icon shows the hotkey window instead of
opening a new one.

To use another workspace, open the workspace switcher, and in that
workspace's <kbd>…</kbd> menu choose **Set as Hotkey Workspace**. The current
hotkey window hides and goes back to being a plain window; the next press
shows the chosen workspace, opening it if it is closed or taking over its
window if it is already open. **Unset Hotkey Workspace** on the current one
leaves its window where it is, and the next press opens a fresh workspace.
The switcher row and the Window menu mark the hotkey workspace with the
hotkey's keys. The items appear only while the global hotkey is on.

Settings → Appearance → **Hotkey window** holds the switches:

| Setting | `config.json` | Does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Global hotkey | `global_hotkey` | On or off; off is `null` |
| Hotkey | `global_hotkey` | Click it, then press the chord. <kbd>Esc</kbd> keeps the old one, <kbd>Backspace</kbd> turns the hotkey off |
| Full screen | `global_hotkey_fullscreen` | Show the hotkey window over the screen the mouse is on, below its menu bar and over the Dock area, above other apps' windows and on the current Space |
| Hide on focus loss | `global_hotkey_hide_on_blur` | Hide the hotkey window whenever you switch to another app |
| Fade | `global_hotkey_fade_ms` | Fade in and out over this many milliseconds; `0` is instant |

The chord uses keymap syntax, as in `keybindings`: `"alt-space"`,
`"cmd-shift-t"`, `"ctrl-\`"`. It has to be a single chord. Edits to
`config.json` take effect without a restart.

In full screen, a tty7 window you open or click while the hotkey window is up
(Settings, a file picker, another workspace) goes above it; one that was
already open stays below until you click it. Clicking the hotkey window puts
it back on top. A switch to another app drops the window back to the normal
level so the other app is not stuck under it; the next hotkey press raises it
again. Turning full screen off puts the window back where it was.

macOS only; the hotkey needs no Accessibility permission. If another app
already holds the chord, tty7 logs that it could not register it.
